# Nature, oceans and environmental science library (`nature`)

Ages 5–14. **Host:** Ripple (new; a round seafoam-green sea-otter-monster with a kelp-leaf hat and a shell she carries everywhere, see `CHARACTERS.md`). Cameos: Hoot (forest), Pico (oceans of the world), Dabble (science), Rumble (slow, patient observer).
**Aligned with:** NGSS life science and earth-and-human-activity performance expectations (nextgenscience.org): K-ESS3-1, K-ESS3-3, 2-LS4-1, 3-LS4-3, 3-LS4-4, 4-LS1-1, 5-LS2-1, 5-ESS3-1, MS-LS2-1 … MS-LS2-5, MS-ESS2-4, MS-ESS3-3, MS-ESS3-5. `MS-LS2-*` and `MS-ESS3-*` were checked on nextgenscience.org (2026-11-17); others are re-checked during validation. Reef facts follow NOAA (https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html).

**Tone rule:** facts first, hopeful agency always. Environmental problems are presented with what people are doing to help, never as doom.

## 3. World concepts (prioritised)

### 1. Ripple's Reef Rescue — **first world** (ages 8–12)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Helper:** Dabble (science explainer) · **Scene:** a coral reef off Monster Island, pale in places after a hot summer.
- **Hook:** Part of Ripple's reef has turned white after a hot summer, and she needs a helper to bring it back to life.
- **Interaction:** drag creatures into food chains, sort help/harm cards, plant coral fragments on a restoration frame, read a temperature chart. **Answer kind:** sequence / choice / set-matching.
- **Levels:** see §4 (9 levels).
- **Hints:** Dabble's zoom lens shows tiny algae inside a coral polyp. **A11y:** all creatures named; chart as table.

### 2. Habitat Homes (ages 5–7)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Hoot · **Scene:** a picture map with forest, pond, desert, ocean and snow.
- **Hook:** Animals got mixed up at the island fair and need to get home.
- **Interaction:** drag animals to habitats. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. fish vs bird
  2. 3 habitats
  3. 5 habitats
  4. why: food, water, shelter
  5. one animal, two possible homes
  6. a habitat change: where do they go?

### 3. Food Web Weaver (ages 8–12)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Hoot · **Scene:** a forest web woven from glowing threads.
- **Hook:** Hoot's forest web of who-eats-whom is tangled, and it must be rewoven.
- **Interaction:** draw arrows from food to eater; remove a species and predict. **Answer kind:** sequence / set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. 3-link chain
  2. arrow means "gives energy to"
  3. producers vs consumers
  4. decomposers
  5. web with 2 paths
  6. remove grazers
  7. two-step effects
  8. matter loops vs energy flow

### 4. Droplet's Big Trip (water cycle) (ages 7–10)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Rumble (naps in a cloud) · **Scene:** ocean, sky, mountain and river.
- **Hook:** A tiny droplet wants to travel the world and come home again.
- **Interaction:** steer the droplet through stages by choosing the process. **Answer kind:** sequence.
- **Levels:**
  1. evaporate
  2. condense
  3. rain/snow
  4. collect in rivers
  5. groundwater
  6. plants release water
  7. full cycle from any start

### 5. Ocean Zones Dive (ages 9–12)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Pico · **Scene:** a bathysphere descending through ocean layers.
- **Hook:** Ripple has always wondered who lives below the light, so she and Pico dive.
- **Interaction:** place animals in zones; match adaptations. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. sunlight zone
  2. twilight
  3. midnight
  4. light and colour fade
  5. glowing animals (bioluminescence)
  6. pressure and cold
  7. food falls from above

### 6. Tide Pool Explorer (ages 6–9)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Scene:** rocky shore at low tide.
- **Hook:** At low tide, little pools hold surprises, and Ripple's field guide is empty.
- **Interaction:** observe with a magnifier; sort by features. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. shell or no shell
  2. legs, arms, none
  3. how it eats
  4. sea star is not a fish
  5. why look, don't take
  6. tide in vs out

### 7. Weather or Climate Lab (ages 10–14)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Zephy · **Scene:** an island weather station with records.
- **Hook:** The island's 100-year weather diary was found in the lighthouse attic.
- **Interaction:** read charts; classify statements; match evidence. **Answer kind:** choice / set-matching / numeric-tolerance (reading values).
- **Levels:**
  1. today's weather vs average
  2. read a bar chart
  3. one cold day doesn't change climate
  4. 30-year averages
  5. trend line
  6. greenhouse blanket model (labelled analogy)
  7. what people are doing (renewable energy, efficiency)
- Data shown are clearly sourced or clearly labelled as illustrative.

### 8. Rewild the Meadow (ages 9–14)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Brix · **Scene:** an old car park that the village wants to turn back into meadow and stream.
- **Hook:** The village has voted to turn a car park into a home for wildlife.
- **Interaction:** place plants, ponds, paths within a budget; check biodiversity score (rule-based). **Answer kind:** set-matching (rule check).
- **Levels:**
  1. native plants
  2. water source
  3. shelter
  4. connect habitats
  5. paths that don't split habitat
  6. compare 2 designs
  7. monitor and improve

### 9. Adaptation Designer (ages 8–11)
- **Host:** Ripple · **Cameo:** Gloop · **Scene:** a "design an animal" lab.
- **Hook:** Gloop wants to imagine creatures for new planets, built from real adaptations.
- **Interaction:** choose body features for an environment; test survival. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. cold
  2. hot desert
  3. dark deep sea
  4. predators
  5. two pressures
  6. environment changes: who survives well, less well, not at all

## 4. First world to build: **Ripple's Reef Rescue**

**Why first:** the owner's example; reefs connect food webs, partnerships, human impact and solutions in one place; it produces a vivid restoration arc (white → colourful) that rewards learning visibly.

### 4.1 Objective and skills
"Child can explain how a reef food chain works, what corals need, why reefs bleach, and which actions help them recover." L0–L1 `nature.habitat.needs` (Lm 1); L2–L3 `nature.web.chain` (Lm 3); L4 `nature.reef.coral` (Lm 4); L5 `nature.reef.bleaching` (Lm 5); L6–L7 `nature.reef.restore` / `nature.web.change` (Lm 6); L8 `nature.reef.restore` (Lm 8); L9 `nature.reef.bleaching` data (Lm 9).

### 4.2 Story

### 4.3 Board
- A side-view reef scene with zones (shallow reef, sandy patch, seagrass). Coral patches show their state: colourful, pale, white.
- Creature cards (original art): parrotfish, surgeonfish, sea urchin, green sea turtle, reef shark, small damselfish, sea cucumber, algae, seagrass. Avoid clownfish (strongly associated with a famous film) to keep art clearly original.
- Modes: **chain** (drag cards into an ordered chain with arrows "gives energy to"), **choice** (tap a card), **sort** (Help / Harm bins), **plant** (drag coral fragments onto frame slots), **chart** (tap a bar on a weekly temperature chart).
- Each correct step brings colour back to one reef patch (state persists through the round, so the reef visibly recovers).

### 4.4 Generator rules (seeded; facts from an authored, reviewed fact table)
| Level | Item | Answer | Validation | Distractor / misconception |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 tutorial | "Parrotfish eats…" | choice (algae) | equality | pointing hand |
| 1 | who eats what (6 facts: parrotfish→algae, surgeonfish→algae, urchin→algae, turtle→seagrass, shark→fish, sea cucumber→sand detritus) | choice of 3 | equality | a food from another animal |
| 2 | 3-link chain: sun → algae → grazer | sequence | exact | reversed arrows (eater before food) |
| 3 | 4-link chain: sun → algae → small fish → reef shark | sequence | exact | grazer placed as top predator |
| 4 | "Coral is a…" (animal) then "Its tiny algae partners give it…" (food and colour) | choice + choice | equality | rock, plant |
| 5 | bleaching cause chain: water gets too warm → coral pushes out its algae → coral turns white → coral is hungry and weak (can recover if water cools) | sequence (4) | exact | "bleach chemicals" card |
| 6 | sort 6 cards: help (less pollution washing into the sea, protecting grazing fish, careful boating/anchoring, coral nurseries, cooler water) vs harm (very warm water, runoff pollution, overfishing grazers, anchors dropped on coral) | set-matching | accepted sets | "touching coral helps it" |
| 7 | algae smothering coral: which animals to protect/return? | set (choose all grazers from 5) | accepted set | top predator only |
| 8 | plant 4 coral fragments on a frame with 8 slots: rule = shallow (light), not in shade of an overhang, not covered by algae, not on sand that shifts | set-matching | rule check | deep dark slots |
| 9 | 12-week sea-temperature bar chart (illustrative values) with a dashed "usual summer high" line: "When was the coral most at risk?" (longest run above the line) / "How many weeks above?" | choice / number | equality | the single hottest week vs the longest run |

Constraints: L1 round covers ≥4 different animals; L6 round has ≥2 help and ≥2 harm cards; L9 charts have a unique longest run, distinct from the hottest single week in ≥50% of items.

### 4.5 Hint ladder
- **First miss:** the wrong card floats back; Dabble's zoom bubble shows the relevant clue (a parrotfish nibbling algae; algae inside a coral polyp; a thermometer over the reef); concept line plays.
- **Second miss (model):** cards move into place one by one with arrows drawn and narrated; the child taps to confirm (errorless).

### 4.6 Helper explanations (Dabble)
- `rr.help.chain` "Arrows show where the energy goes: from the food to the eater."
- `rr.help.sun` "Every food chain starts with the sun. Algae and seagrass use sunlight to make food."
- `rr.help.coral` "Coral is an animal! Tiny algae live inside it and share the food they make."
- `rr.help.bleach` "When water stays too warm, coral pushes out its algae and turns white. If the water cools, it can recover."
- `rr.help.grazers` "Fish and urchins that eat algae keep it from covering the coral."
- `rr.help.plant` "New coral needs sunlight and a clean, steady place to grow."
- `rr.help.chart` "Look for weeks above the dashed line. Many warm weeks in a row are the hardest for coral."

### 4.8 Reward
Sticker **"Reef Guardian"**: a round shell badge with a coral sprig and Ripple's kelp-leaf hat.

### 4.9 Accessibility
Creatures and slots are buttons with names; chart available as a table ("week 5: 30 °C, above usual high"); reef colour states also shown with icons (healthy / pale / white) so colour isn't the only cue; reduced motion: no swimming loops.

## 5. Accuracy notes
- Coral facts follow NOAA: corals are animals; symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) live in their tissues and provide food and colour; heat stress causes corals to expel the algae and turn white; bleached corals are not dead but are under stress and more vulnerable, and can recover if conditions improve.
- Food-chain facts in the authored table are checked by a marine-science educator (e.g. green sea turtles graze seagrass and algae; parrotfish and surgeonfish graze algae).
- Temperature charts in L9 are labelled "example data" unless sourced (if real, cite NOAA Coral Reef Watch and the date).
- Climate content: aligned with MS-ESS3-5's focus on evidence for the rise in global temperatures over the past century; factual, age-appropriate, with solutions; reviewed by a science educator.

## 6. Age-appropriateness and safety
- Hopeful framing: every problem shown is paired with real actions people take (nurseries, protected areas, cleaner water).
- No dead or suffering animals depicted; white coral is shown as "pale and tired", recovering with help.
- No guilt messages aimed at children or families; actions are framed as what communities and scientists do, with small things kids can do.
- Tide-pool and nature content teaches "look, don't take" and staying safe near water with grown-ups.
