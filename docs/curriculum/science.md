# Science experiments and physics library (`sci`)

Ages 5–14. **Host:** Dabble (new; a round coral-red newt-monster in oversized safety goggles whose head-frill puffs up when an idea arrives, see `CHARACTERS.md`). Cameos: Rumble (measures forces with a spring scale), Gloop (states of matter), Zephy (flight cross-over), Brix (engineering cross-over).
**Aligned with:** Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) performance expectations (nextgenscience.org). Codes in the skills table are published NGSS PEs; `5-PS2-1` was checked on nextgenscience.org (2026-11-17), the rest are re-checked during content validation.

**House rule:** all experiments are **virtual**. The game never instructs children to handle heat, flames, electricity from outlets, or chemicals. Optional "try with a grown-up" cards (parent page only) use kitchen-safe materials and say "with adult supervision".

## 3. World concepts (prioritised)

### 1. Dabble's Rocket Lab — **first world** (ages 8–12)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Helper:** Rumble (measures with a giant spring scale) · **Scene:** a launch pad on the island's flat hilltop.
- **Hook:** Dabble wants to send a moon-flower seed to the Moon garden, and each rocket needs the right engine to get off the pad.
- **Interaction:** drag engines and cargo onto the rocket; watch force arrows; launch. **Answer kind:** choice / number / sequence.
- **Levels:** see §4 (8 levels).
- **Hints:** force arrows drawn to scale; Rumble weighs the rocket. **A11y:** forces as numbers and arrows.

### 2. Push & Pull Playground (ages 5–7)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Bobbo · **Scene:** a playground with balls, wagons and ramps.
- **Hook:** Bobbo's ball must reach the goal flag, and only the right-sized push will do it.
- **Interaction:** pull back a push-paddle (3 strengths); predict which goes farther. **Answer kind:** choice.
- **Levels:**
  1. soft vs hard push
  2. push vs pull
  3. heavy vs light wagon
  4. ramp height
  5. change direction with a push
  6. choose the push to stop at a flag
- **Hints:** a trail shows distance. **A11y:** push strength buttons.

### 3. Ice, Water, Steam Kitchen (ages 6–9)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Gloop (squishes between states) · **Scene:** a monster kitchen with a fridge and a (cartoon, unlit) stove dial.
- **Hook:** Gloop's jelly lake keeps freezing and melting, and the party needs it just right.
- **Interaction:** slide a temperature dial; sort changes. **Answer kind:** choice / set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. melt vs freeze
  2. water ↔ steam
  3. reversible sort
  4. cooking can't be undone
  5. particle pictures
  6. same mass after melting
  7. predict state at a temperature
- **Hints:** particle zoom bubble.

### 4. Circuit Glowworms (ages 8–11)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Loopi · **Scene:** a dark cave where glowworms light only in complete circuits.
- **Hook:** The cave party is dark, and the glowworms need complete circuits to shine.
- **Interaction:** drag wires, batteries, switches; test materials. **Answer kind:** set-matching (rule check: closed loop through bulb and battery).
- **Levels:**
  1. close one gap
  2. conductor vs insulator
  3. switch on/off
  4. two bulbs in series
  5. parallel (one stays lit)
  6. find the broken part
  7. motor and buzzer
- Battery voltages are toy-level; never mains electricity.

### 5. Energy Coaster (ages 10–14)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Brix · **Scene:** a wooden coaster park.
- **Hook:** The new coaster must make it over every hill without a motor after the first climb.
- **Interaction:** drag hill heights; predict speeds; watch energy bars. **Answer kind:** choice / sequence.
- **Levels:**
  1. higher start, faster bottom
  2. KE vs PE bars
  3. can it reach the next hill?
  4. friction turns energy to heat
  5. mass vs speed in KE
  6. order hills for a full ride
  7. loop needs extra height
- Energy model labelled simplified.

### 6. Seed to Sprout Greenhouse (ages 5–8)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Hoot · **Scene:** greenhouse with pots.
- **Hook:** Hoot's seeds won't sprout, and each pot is missing something different.
- **Interaction:** give water, light, warmth; order life cycle cards. **Answer kind:** set-matching / sequence.
- **Levels:**
  1. water
  2. light
  3. too much water
  4. seed life cycle
  5. butterfly cycle
  6. frog cycle
  7. plants make their own food from light, air and water (5-LS1-1 idea, simplified)

### 7. Body Builders (ages 7–10)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Cameo:** Rumble · **Scene:** a friendly X-ray "see-through" mirror.
- **Hook:** Rumble wants to know how he can hop, breathe and see, so Dabble builds a model.
- **Interaction:** drag organs/bones to the model; match to functions. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. external parts
  2. bones support
  3. muscles move bones
  4. heart pumps
  5. lungs breathe
  6. senses
  7. systems working together
- Body drawings are simple and non-graphic; no medical advice.

### 8. Fizzy Potion Lab (ages 10–13)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Scene:** a virtual lab bench with labelled, harmless pretend substances.
- **Hook:** Dabble's potions sometimes make new substances and sometimes just mix, and the lab book needs evidence.
- **Interaction:** mix virtual substances; record evidence in a table. **Answer kind:** set-matching / choice.
- **Levels:**
  1. physical vs chemical
  2. gas bubbles as evidence
  3. colour change caveat
  4. temperature change
  5. dissolving is physical
  6. properties before/after
  7. mass conserved in a closed jar
- No real-world mixing instructions; no named hazardous chemicals.

### 9. Cell City (ages 11–14)
- **Host:** Dabble · **Scene:** a city map that is a cell.
- **Hook:** Cell City's power plant, library and walls need the right buildings, just like a cell's parts.
- **Interaction:** place parts; compare plant and animal cells. **Answer kind:** set-matching.
- **Levels:**
  1. nucleus
  2. membrane
  3. mitochondria
  4. cell wall
  5. chloroplasts
  6. plant vs animal
  7. what happens if a part fails
- Analogy labelled as analogy.

## 4. First world to build: **Dabble's Rocket Lab**

**Why first:** the owner's example; forces are central to the physics strand (3-PS2-1 → 5-PS2-1 → MS-PS2-2), the build-and-launch interaction is dramatic and rewarding, and numbers stay small.

### 4.1 Objective and skills
"Child can predict whether a rocket lifts off by comparing thrust with weight, and explain how mass and gravity change that." L0–L3 `sci.force.balanced` (Lm 2); L4–L5 `sci.force.weight-mass` (Lm 5); L6–L8 `sci.force.net` (Lm 6).

### 4.2 Story
| Beat | Line (`rl.`) |
|---|---|
| intro1 | "Dabble found a moon-flower seed. It only grows in the Moon garden!" |
| intro2 | "So Dabble is building rockets. Lots of rockets." |
| intro3 | "A rocket needs a push up that beats the pull down." |
| intro4 | "Help Dabble pick engines and launch the seed!" |
| between2 | "Liftoff! Rumble had to hold his hat." |
| between4 | "Now Dabble wants to carry more cargo." |
| between6 | "Next stop: launching from the Moon itself!" |
| outro1 | "The moon-flower seed landed in the Moon garden!" |
| outro2 | "You compared the push up with the pull down every time." |
| outro3 | "Here's your Launch Patch!" |

### 4.3 Board
- A rocket on a pad, side view. Two arrows from its centre: **thrust** (up, flame-orange) and **weight** (down, deep blue), lengths exactly proportional to their values; numbers on each arrow.
- Engine shelf (3 engines with thrust labels) and cargo crates (fixed weight each). Drag or tap to attach; keyboard: Tab + Enter.
- **Launch** button. If thrust > weight: rocket rises (speed-up shown by spacing of exhaust puffs). If thrust = weight: rocket rumbles but stays ("balanced"). If thrust < weight: engine puffs and the rocket stays on the pad, no explosion ever.
- L4+: a planet switch (Earth / Moon) and a mass label in kg.

### 4.4 Model and units
- L0–L3: game force units ("pushes"), integers 1–20.
- L4+: newtons. **Weight = mass × g** with `g_Earth = 10 N/kg` (labelled "rounded from 9.8") and `g_Moon = 1.6 N/kg`. Masses are multiples of 5 kg so Moon weights are whole numbers.
- L6+: net force `F = T − W`; acceleration `a = F / m` (m/s²), drag ignored and labelled "no air resistance in this lab".

### 4.5 Generator rules (seeded)
| Level | Item | Values | Answer | Distractor rule (misconception) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 tutorial | lift off? | T = 6, W = 3 | choice yes/no | pointing hand |
| 1 | lift off? | W 2–10, T = W ± (2…5) | choice | no distractor beyond yes/no; arrows differ clearly |
| 2 | pick the engine that lifts the rocket | W 4–15; engines {W − k, **W**, W + j} (k, j 1–4), shuffled | choice | the equal engine (balanced = "goes up slowly"); the weaker engine |
| 3 | most cargo crates while still lifting | rocket W0 4–8, crate c ∈ {1, 2, 3}, T fixed 10–20; answer n = max with W0 + n·c < T | number | n where W0 + n·c = T (balanced counted as lift) |
| 4 | weight from mass, then engine | m ∈ {5,10,…,40} kg on Earth → W = 10m; engines {W − 10k, W + 10j, W} | number (weight) then choice | weight = mass (forgetting g) |
| 5 | Moon launch | same m; Moon W = 1.6m; ask "mass on the Moon?" (number = m) then pick the smallest engine that lifts | number + choice | mass changes on the Moon; using the Earth engine size |
| 6 | rank 3 rockets by acceleration | T and m chosen so a ∈ {1…6} m/s², all different, at least one pair where the heavier rocket has a bigger thrust but a smaller acceleration | sequence | "biggest engine is fastest"; "heaviest is fastest" |
| 7 | fuel burns: mass drops from m to m − Δ with same thrust | compute new net force and say faster / slower / same speed-up | number + choice | "same thrust means same acceleration" |
| 8 | mission planner | pre-computed lookup table (engine × fuel) → apogee band; budget limit | set-matching (rule: reaches target band and cost ≤ budget) | biggest engine always best (over budget) |

Constraints (levels × 50 seeds): integers everywhere except Moon weights (multiples of 8 N for m multiples of 5 kg); no ties in rankings; L2 always contains exactly one engine that lifts; L3 answer ≥ 1.

### 4.6 Hint ladder
- **First miss:** Rumble hangs the rocket on his spring scale ("This rocket weighs 12 pushes"), the two arrows pulse side by side, and the wrong choice greys out.
- **Second miss (model):** arrows slide next to each other to compare lengths; the longer one is labelled "wins"; the correct engine glows; the child taps it (errorless) and launches.

### 4.7 Helper explanations (Rumble)
- `rl.help.start` "The engine pushes up. Gravity pulls down. The bigger one wins."
- `rl.help.balanced` "Equal pushes cancel out. The rocket stays still."
- `rl.help.mass` "Mass is how much stuff. Weight is how hard gravity pulls on it."
- `rl.help.moon` "The Moon pulls more gently, so the same rocket weighs less there. Its mass stays the same."
- `rl.help.net` "Take the pull from the push. What's left speeds the rocket up."
- `rl.help.fuel` "Burning fuel makes the rocket lighter, so the same push speeds it up more."

### 4.9 Reward
Sticker **"Launch Patch"**: a round mission patch with Dabble's goggles and a moon-flower.

### 4.10 Accessibility
Arrow values are text; launch outcome announced ("The rocket stays on the pad: the push and pull are equal"); reduced motion shows start and end positions only; colour of arrows backed by labels "push up" / "pull down".

## 5. Accuracy notes
- Physics simplifications are labelled in the UI: g rounded to 10 N/kg; no air resistance; constant thrust.
- Moon surface gravity ≈ 1.6 N/kg (1.62 m/s²); Earth ≈ 9.8 N/kg. Check against a NASA fact sheet during validation.
- "Balanced forces" means no change in motion (an object at rest stays at rest), not "slow movement".
- Avoid "there is no gravity in space": astronauts float because they are in free fall. This appears only in band D notes.
- A science educator reviews each world before release.

## 6. Age-appropriateness and safety
- No explosions, injuries or failures shown as disasters; failed launches just stay on the pad.
- No real-world experiment instructions for children; parent cards say "with adult supervision" and use only kitchen-safe materials.
- Body content is anatomical and non-graphic, with no diagnosis or health advice.
